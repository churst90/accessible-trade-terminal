## Method in its own terms

Alex trades **trend continuation with trigger waves**. First "identify the trend": in an uptrend the waves "typically" live above the 50 line, and dips back to zero are continuation. A **trigger wave** is a small momentum wave that **does not reach the overbought/oversold lines**: momentum "dips its toes" across the zero line and punches back in the trend's direction. Tighter and more compact is better. A full swing down to oversold and back is a different thing, and he ignores it. He "hunts" on the 4H (the 6H is "going to rip"). Waiting for the 4H dot to print and confirm is "too late", so he **front-runs** it by watching the 1H, 2H and 3H confirm their dots first while the VWAP "pulls" the momentum wave like a rope. He enters on the retest, with "good risk management".

## Candidate rules NOT in the pre-registered setup grader

1. **Shallow-trigger continuation long.** Trend up (for example close > EMA50 > EMA200, or WT mostly > 0 over the last N bars; the trend proxy is ours). A WT pullback with min(WT2) between −60 and about 0 (never reaching oversold). Enter on the WT1-over-WT2 cross. This is the **opposite depth** to the grader's WT2 ≤ −60 dot, so it is a distinct setup. Computable on daily bars. Terminal: Cipher B.
2. **Lower-timeframe dot cascade as an early entry.** Enter when the dots on T/4, T/2 and 3T/4 have printed in order and the T dot has not yet printed. Needs intraday data (1H, 2H, 3H for a 4H hunt) that we may not have. A daily analogue: daily + 2D + 3D dots before the weekly one.
3. **Compactness.** Rank trigger waves by width (bars below zero) and depth, and test whether "tighter" outperforms. Computable.
