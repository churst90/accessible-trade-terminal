## Method in its own terms

The basic strategy tries "to capitalize on a shift of momentum and money flow" after both have become "overextended" on one side ([Basic Strategy, 2023-11-24, 11:47](https://youtu.be/sYJD_E6pXV4?t=707)). The sequence is:
1. an **anchor wave**: a momentum wave past ±60;
2. **money flow showing clear direction**: in the red (for longs) as the anchor prints, then curving back toward zero;
3. a **trigger wave**: a smaller wave in the new direction, with a higher low.

Conviction rises with:
- deep, well-established money flow that curves smoothly;
- a deep, thick anchor and a trigger "tight to the zero line";
- MC B's two RSI lines tight together and hugging the bottom;
- above all, the **VWAP curving inward with increasing sharpness as it crosses zero** while the trigger wave prints. That is "the cherry on top".

Neighbouring timeframes (two up, two down) should back it up. He plans the stop, targets and de-risking in advance. He risks 0.5 to 1% per trade and exits on structure breaks, at levels, or when money flow becomes thick on the other side and price slows.

## Anchor wave, trigger wave, money flow and confirmation (exact)

- **Anchor wave (exact).** "an anchor wave is just going to be a wave that is very overextended to one side". He judges that against the ±60 lines: "in general if a wave gets past the -60 line or the 60 line I will consider it an anchor wave" ([Basic Strategy, 2023-11-24, 12:49](https://youtu.be/sYJD_E6pXV4?t=769); [Basic Strategy, 2023-11-24, 13:20](https://youtu.be/sYJD_E6pXV4?t=800)). It does not say which line (light or dark wave) must pass −60. No dotted line is mentioned.
  - **Deeper is better.** A "deep anchor wave", e.g. "well below the -60 line" with big green dots, shows "a bit more strength" ([Basic Strategy, 2023-11-24, 18:58](https://youtu.be/sYJD_E6pXV4?t=1138); [Basic Strategy, 2023-11-24, 19:59](https://youtu.be/sYJD_E6pXV4?t=1199)).
  - **Thicker is better** (time spent extended), but he has not backtested this: "I haven't really back tested the effect of thickness on the momentum waves". "the quicker and more Jagged the momentum waves are probably the less confidence I would have" ([Basic Strategy, 2023-11-24, 21:31](https://youtu.be/sYJD_E6pXV4?t=1291); [Basic Strategy, 2023-11-24, 22:01](https://youtu.be/sYJD_E6pXV4?t=1321)).
- **Trigger wave (exact).**
  - It is "simply ... a wave that shows direction from the anchor wave". "this wave is clearly much smaller than the anchor wave" ([Basic Strategy, 2023-11-24, 14:21](https://youtu.be/sYJD_E6pXV4?t=861); [Basic Strategy, 2023-11-24, 14:51](https://youtu.be/sYJD_E6pXV4?t=891)).
  - **The defining test is a higher low (for longs).** A second wave that also goes below −60 still counts as a trigger, because "it is still putting in a higher low compared to this wave" ([Basic Strategy, 2023-11-24, 15:23](https://youtu.be/sYJD_E6pXV4?t=923)).
  - A wave can be both a trigger to the earlier anchor and an anchor to a later, tighter trigger. One anchor can have many triggers ("seven or eight"), measured from the original anchor ([Basic Strategy, 2023-11-24, 15:54](https://youtu.be/sYJD_E6pXV4?t=954); [Basic Strategy, 2023-11-24, 16:24](https://youtu.be/sYJD_E6pXV4?t=984)).
  - **Ideal: a trigger "tight to the zero line".** In his parentheses, "good direction" matters more than tightness ([Basic Strategy, 2023-11-24, 18:58](https://youtu.be/sYJD_E6pXV4?t=1138); [Basic Strategy, 2023-11-24, 19:29](https://youtu.be/sYJD_E6pXV4?t=1169)).
  - After momentum has crossed back above zero, a "really tight trigger wave" is "a pretty solid ... confirmation for continuation" ([Basic Strategy, 2023-11-24, 16:56](https://youtu.be/sYJD_E6pXV4?t=1016); [Basic Strategy, 2023-11-24, 17:26](https://youtu.be/sYJD_E6pXV4?t=1046)).
- **Invalidation of the anchor→trigger sequence.** If momentum "prints a lower wave" (a lower low below the trigger), "there's a good chance that this momentum shift is over". That wave becomes a new anchor, and you wait for a new build-up ([Basic Strategy, 2023-11-24, 16:24](https://youtu.be/sYJD_E6pXV4?t=984)). He hedges: a lower low under one trigger can still leave a later wave as a trigger to the original anchor. "the better the trend line of momentum that you can draw ... the better strength" ([Basic Strategy, 2023-11-24, 16:56](https://youtu.be/sYJD_E6pXV4?t=1016)). **So the hard break is a low below the anchor's low. A low below a trigger only weakens the setup.** (This is our reading of his two statements.)
- **Time between anchor and trigger.** Nothing stated in bars. In his example "money flow was still decreasing for a while after we put in that anchor wave" ([Basic Strategy, 2023-11-24, 14:21](https://youtu.be/sYJD_E6pXV4?t=861)).
- **Money flow colour, shape, depth (exact).**
  - *At the anchor:* "as the anchor wave prints you want the money flow to be either already in the red or coming down into the red pretty significantly" (mirror for shorts) ([Basic Strategy, 2023-11-24, 13:20](https://youtu.be/sYJD_E6pXV4?t=800); [Basic Strategy, 2023-11-24, 13:51](https://youtu.be/sYJD_E6pXV4?t=831)).
  - *Afterwards:* "as time progresses you want to see the money flow begin to Trend towards the zero line essentially showing like a curvature" ([Basic Strategy, 2023-11-24, 13:51](https://youtu.be/sYJD_E6pXV4?t=831)).
  - *"Deep" money flow (exact as he gets).* "you want to see deep money flow ... a kind of deep retracement". Not "a really tight money flow wave in the red" ([Basic Strategy, 2023-11-24, 17:57](https://youtu.be/sYJD_E6pXV4?t=1077)). It should be "well established on one side of the zero line" and have "been in the red for a while" ([Basic Strategy, 2023-11-24, 18:28](https://youtu.be/sYJD_E6pXV4?t=1108)). In short: deep on one side and curving toward zero ([Basic Strategy, 2023-11-24, 18:58](https://youtu.be/sYJD_E6pXV4?t=1138)). **No number** for depth or duration.
  - *Bad money flow:* "Jagged", the "teeth effect" (spikes up that fail to cross zero), flattening with slightly lower lows ([Basic Strategy, 2023-11-24, 22:31](https://youtu.be/sYJD_E6pXV4?t=1351); [Basic Strategy, 2023-11-24, 23:01](https://youtu.be/sYJD_E6pXV4?t=1381)). Also shallow, short-lived red ([Basic Strategy, 2023-11-24, 21:00](https://youtu.be/sYJD_E6pXV4?t=1260)).
  - All of this is on the setup timeframe, with neighbouring timeframes checked as well (see Context).
- **What he WAITS FOR after a green dot prints (exact).**
  - (a) **The trigger wave itself.** In his best example, "it wasn't until these trigger waves printed that a good trade setup was actually presented". He puts a vertical line where they printed, and "either of those would have been a decent entry" ([Basic Strategy, 2023-11-24, 24:31](https://youtu.be/sYJD_E6pXV4?t=1471)). The anchor's own dot (even a big one) is not the entry.
  - (b) **Money flow curving up as the trigger prints.** In that example it "actually crossed into the green when this trigger wave printed" ([Basic Strategy, 2023-11-24, 24:01](https://youtu.be/sYJD_E6pXV4?t=1441)).
  - (c) **VWAP curvature as the trigger's dot forms.** This is "the final confirmation essentially" ([Basic Strategy, 2023-11-24, 30:09](https://youtu.be/sYJD_E6pXV4?t=1809)).
  - If money flow is still falling when the trigger prints, "I would not call this a signal" ([Basic Strategy, 2023-11-24, 33:43](https://youtu.be/sYJD_E6pXV4?t=2023)). In a borderline case "maybe ... you would have waited for the money flow to show more clear Direction" ([Basic Strategy, 2023-11-24, 34:44](https://youtu.be/sYJD_E6pXV4?t=2084)).
  - No candle-close rule is stated.
- **VWAP curvature rule (exact as he gets).** Dots print when the VWAP crosses zero: "Green Dot vwap crossed Red Dot vwap crossed" ([Basic Strategy, 2023-11-24, 29:37](https://youtu.be/sYJD_E6pXV4?t=1777)). Measure the VWAP's angle bar by bar from its low to the zero cross. "you're looking for every tick of the vwap to be increasing in uh sharpness" ([Basic Strategy, 2023-11-24, 30:40](https://youtu.be/sYJD_E6pXV4?t=1840)). In his 1H example there were three bars from the VWAP low to the cross. The angle sharpened from bar 1 to bar 2, then curved "outwards slightly" on the last bar, "a slight sign of weakness" ([Basic Strategy, 2023-11-24, 31:10](https://youtu.be/sYJD_E6pXV4?t=1870); [Basic Strategy, 2023-11-24, 31:40](https://youtu.be/sYJD_E6pXV4?t=1900); [Basic Strategy, 2023-11-24, 32:10](https://youtu.be/sYJD_E6pXV4?t=1930)). You want "inwards curvature" whether at the top or the bottom. A clearly outward curve "basically got rejected before it even crossed the zero line" ([Basic Strategy, 2023-11-24, 32:43](https://youtu.be/sYJD_E6pXV4?t=1963); [Basic Strategy, 2023-11-24, 33:13](https://youtu.be/sYJD_E6pXV4?t=1993)).
- **How a dot is "confirmed".** Nothing stated beyond the VWAP and money flow checks above.
- **Timeframe pairing.** For a 1H setup he checks "a few time frames up", the 2H and 4H, and lower ones, the 30m and 15m ([Basic Strategy, 2023-11-24, 04:37](https://youtu.be/sYJD_E6pXV4?t=277); [Basic Strategy, 2023-11-24, 05:08](https://youtu.be/sYJD_E6pXV4?t=308)).

## Vetoes (things that cancel a trade whatever else says)

- **Money flow still decreasing at the trigger** → "I would not call this a signal", even with good VWAP curvature ([Basic Strategy, 2023-11-24, 33:43](https://youtu.be/sYJD_E6pXV4?t=2023)).
- **A lower momentum low** (below the trigger, and certainly below the anchor) → "this momentum shift is over" ([Basic Strategy, 2023-11-24, 16:24](https://youtu.be/sYJD_E6pXV4?t=984)).
- Soft negatives, not vetoes:
  - RSI hugging the *high* end at the time of a long trigger: "not a great sign" ([Basic Strategy, 2023-11-24, 34:14](https://youtu.be/sYJD_E6pXV4?t=2054));
  - shallow or short money flow; RSIs "all over the place" ([Basic Strategy, 2023-11-24, 21:00](https://youtu.be/sYJD_E6pXV4?t=1260));
  - jagged momentum ([Basic Strategy, 2023-11-24, 22:01](https://youtu.be/sYJD_E6pXV4?t=1321)).

## Candidate rules NOT in the pre-registered setup grader

1. **Anchor → trigger sequence (computable).** Use waves delimited by WT2 sign changes or by WT1/WT2 cross pairs.
   - Anchor = a wave whose min(WT2) < −60, with MF ≤ 0 or falling at its low.
   - Trigger = a later wave with min(WT2_trigger) > min(WT2_anchor) (a higher low) that has not yet made a lower low than the anchor.
   - Entry = the trigger's WT1-over-WT2 cross, with MF(t) > MF(t−k) (curving up).
   - Invalidation = any wave low below the anchor's low, which resets the anchor.
   - Variants: trigger "tight to zero" (min WT2_trigger > −30, threshold ours); anchor "thick" (bars with WT2 < −60 ≥ n).

   The grader enters on the *first* deep cross. Dylan enters on the *later, shallower* one. Computable on daily bars. Terminal: Cipher B.
2. **VWAP curvature into the cross.** Let V = MC B VWAP (in VuManChu, WT1 − WT2). From V's last local minimum to its zero up-cross, require each bar's ΔV to be ≥ the previous bar's ΔV (monotone acceleration). Variant: no deceleration on the final bar. Computable.
3. **Deep, established money flow before the trigger.** MF < 0 for ≥ N consecutive bars with min(MF) ≤ some quantile, then MF rising into the trigger. N and the depth are not stated, so they need pre-registered values.
4. **RSI convergence.** |RSI − StochRSI| small (lines "tight together") and both near the bottom of their range at a long trigger. The MC B RSI mapping is not public, so test it with the terminal's RSI/Stochastic as a proxy. Mark it as a proxy.
5. **Exit when money flow is "thick" on the other side and price slows.** For example, exit a long when MF > 0, MF has fallen from its local max, and the close-to-close change has shrunk for 2 bars. The thresholds are ours. Compare with the grader's exits.
6. **Neighbour-timeframe agreement** (two above, two below, all with WT rising / MF rising at the trigger). Computable with several timeframes, but heavy.
